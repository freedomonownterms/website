A 60-page report lands in your inbox. A research paper you need for an assignment. A contract you're supposed to sign. Reading every word takes hours, but skimming means missing things that matter.

AI can read the whole document and give you the key points in seconds. Used carefully, it's one of the most useful everyday things AI can do. Used carelessly, it can leave you confidently wrong about what a document says. This guide shows you both sides.

## Step 1: Choose your tool

| Tool | Best for |
|---|---|
| **ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Copilot** | Summarising one document quickly and asking questions about it |
| **NotebookLM** (free, from Google) | Working across several documents, with answers linked to the exact passage in your sources |

Most major assistants let you **upload a PDF** on their free plans, with limits on file size and how many uploads you can do. Look for the paperclip or **+** button next to the message box. If uploading isn't available, you can copy and paste the text instead.

## Step 2: Ask for the right kind of summary

"Summarise this" works, but you'll get a generic overview. Tell the AI **why you're reading** the document and you'll get a far more useful summary.

**A quick overview:**
> Summarise this document in 5 bullet points, then give me a one-sentence takeaway. Who is it written for, and what's the main argument or purpose?

**Section by section:**
> Give me a section-by-section summary of this document, with 2–3 bullet points per section. Keep the original section headings.

**For a decision:**
> I need to decide whether to [goal, e.g. approve this proposal / sign this agreement / use this research]. Summarise only the parts of this document that matter for that decision, including risks, costs, deadlines and anything unusual.

**For studying:**
> Summarise this chapter as revision notes with headings, key definitions and formulas, then give me 5 questions that could come up in an exam.

**For a contract or terms:**
> Explain this agreement in plain English. List what I'm agreeing to, what I'm paying, the length of the commitment, how to cancel, and any terms that are unusual or one-sided.

## Step 3: Ask follow-up questions

The real power of AI isn't the summary. It's being able to **ask the document questions**:

- "What does it say about [topic]?"
- "What evidence supports the main conclusion?"
- "Are there any deadlines or dates I need to know?"
- "What are the weaknesses or limitations the authors mention?"
- "Explain the table on page [number] in simple terms."

## Step 4: Check the summary is accurate

This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that matters most. AI summaries can **leave out important details** or occasionally **state things the document doesn't say**.

Ask the AI to show its evidence:

> For each point in your summary, give the page number or section and a short direct quote from the document that supports it.

Then **open the document and check the quotes**, especially for anything you'll act on, quote or rely on. NotebookLM makes this easier because its answers link to the exact passage in your source.

**Always read the original yourself for:**
- Legal agreements and contracts before you sign
- Medical, financial or safety information
- Anything you'll quote or cite in your own work

## Handling very long or tricky documents

**Very long documents:** if the file is too large or the summary feels thin, split it up. Summarise chapter by chapter, then ask for an overall summary of your chapter summaries.

**Scanned PDFs:** if your PDF is a scan or photo rather than real text, the AI has to read the images. Many tools can, but accuracy varies, especially with handwriting, small print and tables. Check carefully.

**Tables and numbers:** ask the AI to extract tables into a clean table you can copy, then compare a few numbers against the original.

**Other languages:** you can ask for a summary in your own language: *"Summarise this document in [language]."*

## A note on privacy

Uploading a document to an AI tool sends it to that company's servers. Before you upload:

- **Don't upload confidential work documents** unless your employer allows that tool.
- **Remove personal details** such as ID numbers, bank details and addresses where you can.
- **Check your tool's settings.** Many let you turn off chat history or the use of your data for training.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can free AI tools summarise PDFs?**
Yes. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot and NotebookLM all handle PDFs on their free plans, with limits on file size and daily use.

**How long a document can AI summarise?**
It depends on the tool, and limits keep growing. Many assistants can handle documents of hundreds of pages, but for very long files, splitting them into sections gives better, more detailed summaries.

**Can I trust an AI summary?**
Trust it as a starting point, not a final answer. Ask for quotes and page numbers, and check anything important against the original.
