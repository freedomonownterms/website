Making a presentation usually means hours of staring at blank slides, hunting for images and fiddling with layouts. AI can now handle most of that. But a deck that AI makes on its own tends to be **generic, wordy and forgettable**.

The best results come from splitting the job: **you** decide the message, **AI** helps with the structure, wording and design, and **you** finish it off. Here's the workflow.

## Step 1: Plan the message first (5 minutes)

Before any slides, get clear on three things. Ask your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Copilot) to help:

> I'm giving a [length]-minute presentation to [audience] about [topic]. The goal is to [inform / persuade / teach / get approval for something]. Ask me questions to work out the single most important message and the 3 key points that support it.

A presentation with one clear message and three supporting points is easier to follow and remember than one with fifteen loosely connected slides.

## Step 2: Get an outline

> Create a slide-by-slide outline for a [length]-minute presentation to [audience]. Main message: [message]. Key points: [points]. For each slide, give a short headline that states the point (not just a topic), 2–4 brief bullet points, and a suggestion for a visual. Include a title slide, an opening hook and a closing slide with a clear call to action.

**Headlines that state the point** make a big difference. Compare "Sales results" with "Sales grew 18% after we changed our pricing". The second tells the audience what to think.

Review the outline and change anything that doesn't fit. It's much easier to fix an outline than finished slides.

## Step 3: Turn the outline into slides

You have several options, depending on the tools you use:

| Option | How it works | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Gamma** | Paste your outline or a prompt and it generates a full, designed deck | Free plan available with limited AI credits; you can export to PowerPoint or PDF |
| **Canva** | Use its AI design features or pick a template and paste in your content | Free plan includes limited AI use; huge template library |
| **PowerPoint with Copilot** | Ask Copilot to build a deck from a prompt or a Word document | Needs a Microsoft 365 plan that includes Copilot |
| **Google Slides with Gemini** | Use Gemini to create slides and images inside Slides | Depends on your Google account or Workspace plan |
| **Any free template** | Paste your AI outline into a simple PowerPoint, Google Slides or Keynote template | Free, and you keep full control |

Features and free limits change often, so check each tool's current plans. **If you're new to this, start with Gamma or a simple template.**

## Step 4: Make it yours

AI-generated decks have a recognisable look: too much text, generic stock images and every slide in the same layout. Fix that with a quick edit:

- **Cut the words.** If a slide has more than about 30 words, move the detail into your speaker notes.
- **One idea per slide.** Split crowded slides.
- **Use real visuals:** your own photos, screenshots, charts from your real data, or simple icons.
- **Check every fact, number and name.** AI can invent statistics that sound plausible.
- **Match your brand or school style** if you have one: fonts, colours, logo.

**About AI images:** they're great for illustrations, but check the tool's terms for how you're allowed to use them, avoid images of real people or brands, and never use them to show something as real when it isn't.

## Step 5: Write speaker notes

> Write speaker notes for each slide of this presentation. Keep them conversational, about [number] seconds per slide, and include a smooth transition to the next slide. Slides: [paste outline or slide text]

Use the notes as a guide, not a script to read word for word. Audiences can tell.

## Step 6: Rehearse with AI

AI can help you practise, too:

> Act as a member of my audience: [describe them]. I'll paste my presentation script. Tell me which parts are unclear or boring, then ask me the 5 toughest questions I'm likely to get at the end.

Then practise out loud, ideally with a timer. Most people run over time, so aim to finish a minute early.

## A 30-minute workflow

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Clarify the message and key points with AI |
| 5 min | Generate and edit the outline |
| 5 min | Build the slides in your tool of choice |
| 10 min | Cut text, fix visuals, check facts |
| 5 min | Generate speaker notes and prepare for questions |

Then spend whatever time you have left rehearsing. It's the most valuable part.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the best free AI presentation maker?**
For a complete deck from a prompt, Gamma is a popular starting point. Canva is strong for design. If you already use PowerPoint or Google Slides at work or school, check which AI features your plan includes.

**Can people tell a presentation was made with AI?**
Often, yes: too much text, generic images and a "template" feel give it away. A few minutes of editing with your own content and visuals fixes that.

**Can I use AI to make a presentation for school?**
Check your school's rules first. Many allow AI for design and outlining but expect the research and ideas to be your own.
