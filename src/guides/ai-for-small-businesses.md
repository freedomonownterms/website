If you run a small business, you're probably the marketer, customer service team, bookkeeper, recruiter and strategist all at once. AI won't run your business for you, but it can take a surprising amount of everyday work off your plate, often for free.

Here are ten practical tasks you can start handing to AI today, with prompts you can copy. They work with ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot and other assistants. Replace the parts in **[brackets]** with your own details.

**Tip:** start each conversation by describing your business. The more the AI knows, the better its answers:

> I run [type of business] in [city/country]. Our customers are mostly [describe them]. We're known for [what makes you different]. Our tone is [friendly / professional / playful]. Keep this in mind for everything I ask.

## 1. Reply to customer messages faster

> Write a friendly, helpful reply to this customer message. Answer their question, apologise once if something went wrong, and offer a clear next step. Keep it under 100 words. Message: [paste]

Save your best replies as templates for the questions you get most often.

## 2. Create a week of social media posts

> Create 7 social media posts for [platform] for my [business]. Mix tips, behind-the-scenes, customer stories and one promotion for [offer]. Include a caption, a suggested image idea and 3–5 relevant hashtags for each.

Then add your own photos. Real pictures of your shop, products and team usually perform better than stock images.

## 3. Write product and service descriptions

> Write a product description for [product] for my online store. Key features: [list]. Who it's for: [customer]. Focus on benefits, not just features. Write a short version (under 50 words) and a longer version (about 150 words).

## 4. Draft marketing emails and offers

> Write a short marketing email announcing [offer / new product / event] to our existing customers. Include a catchy subject line (give me 3 options), one clear call to action, and a friendly sign-off from [your name].

## 5. Answer your FAQs once, properly

> Here are the questions customers ask us most often: [list]. Write clear, friendly answers for an FAQ page on our website. Ask me for any information you need instead of guessing.

The last instruction is important: you don't want AI inventing your return policy or opening hours.

## 6. Translate for customers who speak other languages

> Translate this message into [language], keeping it friendly and natural rather than word-for-word: [paste]

For anything important, such as contracts, safety information or legal notices, have a native speaker or professional check the translation.

## 7. Get help with spreadsheets

> I have a spreadsheet with these columns: [list column names]. Write a formula for [Excel / Google Sheets] that [what you want, e.g. totals sales by month / flags overdue invoices / calculates profit margin]. Explain how to use it.

You can also paste anonymised data and ask: "What trends or problems do you notice in this data?"

## 8. Handle admin writing

AI is excellent at the writing nobody enjoys:

- Polite payment reminders for overdue invoices
- Supplier emails and quote requests
- Job adverts and interview questions
- Simple staff guides and checklists
- Meeting agendas and summaries

> Write a polite but firm reminder to a customer whose invoice of [amount] was due on [date]. This is the [first / second] reminder. Keep it professional and friendly.

## 9. Research and plan

> I'm thinking about [idea, e.g. opening on Sundays / launching a delivery service / raising prices by 10%]. Help me think it through. Ask me questions first, then list the pros, cons, risks and costs to consider, and suggest a small, low-risk way to test it.

> What are common ways businesses like mine [attract more customers / increase repeat visits / reduce costs]? Give me 10 ideas, ranked by effort and likely impact.

## 10. Draft documents (then get them checked)

AI can produce a first draft of documents like your website's About page, a simple business plan, a staff handbook outline or a refund policy:

> Draft a simple, customer-friendly refund policy for my [type of business] in [country]. Our rules are: [your actual rules]. Use plain language.

**Important:** for contracts, terms and conditions, privacy policies and anything legal, financial or tax-related, use AI only for a first draft and **have a qualified professional review it**. Laws differ between countries, and AI can get them wrong.

## Five rules for using AI safely in business

1. **Protect customer data.** Don't paste customers' names, contact details, payment information or private messages into public AI tools. Remove or replace them first, and follow your local data protection laws.
2. **Check everything before it goes out.** AI can state wrong prices, invented facts or promises you can't keep. You're responsible for what you publish and send.
3. **Keep your voice.** Edit AI drafts so they sound like your business, not like every other business using AI.
4. **Be honest with customers.** If a customer thinks they're talking to a person, don't let a bot pretend otherwise. Some places have rules about disclosing AI use.
5. **Use business plans for business data.** If your team uses AI heavily, consider business versions of these tools, which typically offer stronger privacy controls.

## Where to start

Don't try everything at once. Pick the **one task that eats most of your time each week**, such as customer replies, social posts or invoices, and use AI for just that for two weeks. Once it's saving you time, add the next one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is free AI good enough for a small business?**
For most of the tasks above, yes. Consider a paid plan if you hit usage limits, need stronger privacy controls, or use AI daily across a team.

**Which AI tool is best for business?**
ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot are all capable. If you use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, the assistant built into those tools may be most convenient. See our [comparison of ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude](/guides/chatgpt-vs-gemini-vs-claude/).

**Will customers mind that I use AI?**
Most won't, as long as the help is accurate, personal and honest. What customers dislike is generic, wrong or evasive answers, whoever wrote them.
