Two people can ask the same AI for help and get completely different results. The difference is almost never the AI. It's the **prompt**: the instructions you type.

The good news is that writing good prompts isn't a technical skill. It's the same skill as briefing a capable new colleague: say what you want, give the background, and explain what "good" looks like. This guide gives you a simple formula, real examples and templates you can reuse.

Everything here works with ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot and other AI assistants.

## Why short prompts get weak answers

When you type "write a birthday message", the AI has to guess everything: who it's for, how close you are, how long it should be, whether it should be funny. So it plays it safe and gives you something generic.

**Every detail you leave out, the AI fills with something average.** Good prompts remove the guesswork.

## The five-part formula

You don't need all five parts every time, but the more you include, the better the result.

| Part | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Role** | Who should the AI act as? | "Act as an experienced travel planner." |
| **2. Task** | What exactly do you want? | "Plan a 3-day trip to Lisbon." |
| **3. Context** | What background does it need? | "Two adults, first visit, love food and history, moderate budget, no car." |
| **4. Format** | How should the answer look? | "A day-by-day table with morning, afternoon and evening." |
| **5. Constraints** | Any rules or limits? | "Keep walking under 10 km a day. Include one rainy-day option." |

## Before and after

**Weak prompt:**
> Write an email to my landlord about the heating.

**Strong prompt:**
> Write a short, polite but firm email to my landlord, Mr Daniel Okafor. The heating in my flat has not worked for 5 days, and I reported it by phone last Monday. Ask him to arrange a repair by Friday and to confirm a time. Keep it under 120 words and don't sound angry.

The second prompt takes 30 seconds longer to write and saves you several rounds of editing.

## Seven habits that improve any prompt

**1. Paste the material.** If you want a summary, a reply or feedback, paste the text in. The AI can't read your mind or your inbox.

**2. Give an example.** Showing beats describing: "Write it in a style like this: [paste an example]".

**3. Ask for options.** "Give me 3 versions" lets you pick the best one instead of settling for the first one.

**4. Ask it to ask you questions.** For anything complicated, add: *"Before you answer, ask me any questions you need."* This one sentence often improves results more than anything else.

**5. Say who it's for.** "Explain it for a 12-year-old", "for my non-technical manager" or "for an expert" changes everything about the answer.

**6. Set the length.** "In 3 bullet points", "under 100 words" or "one page" stops rambling.

**7. Treat it as a conversation.** The first answer is a draft. Reply with what to change, as you would with a person.

## Follow-ups that fix most answers

Save these. They work on almost any answer:

- "Make it shorter and simpler."
- "Make it sound more like me. Here's something I wrote: [paste]"
- "What's missing or weak in this? Be honest."
- "Give me 3 different versions."
- "Turn this into a table."
- "Explain why you made these choices."
- "Check your answer for mistakes or anything you may have made up."

## Templates you can reuse

**To learn something:**
> Explain [topic] to me as a beginner, using an everyday example. Then ask me 3 questions to check I understood.

**To write something:**
> Write a [type of text] for [audience] about [topic]. Tone: [friendly / formal / persuasive]. Length: [length]. Include: [key points]. Avoid: [things to avoid].

**To decide something:**
> Help me decide between [option A] and [option B]. My priorities are [list]. First ask me any questions you need, then compare them in a table and give a recommendation.

**To improve something:**
> Here's my [draft / plan / idea]. Give me honest feedback on [what you care about], then list the 3 changes that would improve it most: [paste]

**To plan something:**
> Create a step-by-step plan to [goal] in [time frame]. I can spend [time / budget]. Point out the hardest parts and how to handle them.

## Common mistakes

1. **Asking several unrelated things at once.** Split them into separate messages.
2. **Being vague about what "good" means.** "Make it better" is hard to act on; "make it more persuasive for a busy manager" isn't.
3. **Starting a new chat when you should continue.** The AI remembers the conversation, so build on it.
4. **Trusting facts without checking.** AI can sound confident and still be wrong, especially about numbers, dates, quotes and sources.
5. **Pasting private information.** Leave out passwords, ID numbers, bank details and confidential work data.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to learn "prompt engineering"?**
No. For everyday use, clear instructions plus the five-part formula are all you need.

**Do I need to be polite to AI?**
It's not required, but writing naturally, as you would to a helpful colleague, tends to produce clear prompts, and clear prompts get better answers.

**Do the same prompts work in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude?**
Yes. The assistants have different strengths, but a well-written prompt works well in all of them.
