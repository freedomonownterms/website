There are thousands of "AI tools for students" online, and most of them are thin wrappers around the same few AI models, with a paywall on top. You don't need them. A handful of free, well-known tools cover almost everything a student needs.

Here's our shortlist, grouped by what you need help with. All of these have a **free plan**, although free plans have usage limits and features change often, so check each tool's website for the current details. *Last reviewed: November 2026.*

## AI assistants: your all-round tutor

You only need **one** of these. Try two or three, then stick with the one you like.

### ChatGPT
The best-known AI assistant, good at explanations, brainstorming, practice questions and feedback on writing. Its **Study mode** turns it into a tutor that guides you with questions and hints instead of handing over answers.
**Best for:** all-round studying and practice.

### Google Gemini
Google's assistant, built into the Google apps many schools already use. Its **Guided Learning** feature breaks topics into steps and can add visuals, videos and quizzes.
**Best for:** students who live in Google Docs, Drive and Gmail.

### Claude
Particularly strong at reading long documents and giving thoughtful, well-written feedback. It has a question-led **learning mode**, and its projects let you upload course materials to work from.
**Best for:** long readings, essays and writing feedback.

### Microsoft Copilot
Free and built into Windows and the Edge browser. It's a natural choice if your school uses Microsoft 365, where Copilot features may also appear inside Word and PowerPoint, depending on your school's plan.
**Best for:** students in Microsoft-based schools.

## Studying from your own material

### NotebookLM
A free Google tool that answers **only from the sources you upload**, such as lecture slides, PDFs, notes, websites or YouTube videos, and shows which source each answer came from. It can generate flashcards, quizzes, study guides and podcast-style audio overviews of your material.
**Best for:** revising from your own course content. See our guide to [turning notes into flashcards and quizzes](/guides/turn-notes-into-flashcards-and-quizzes-with-ai/).

### Anki
Not an AI tool, but the perfect partner for one. Anki is a free flashcard app that uses **spaced repetition**, showing each card just before you'd forget it. Let AI draft your cards, then review them in Anki. (Free on most platforms; the iPhone app is paid.)
**Best for:** memorising anything: vocabulary, formulas, definitions, dates.

## Research

### Perplexity
An AI search engine that answers questions with **links to its sources**, which makes it a good starting point for research. Always open the sources and check they say what the summary claims.
**Best for:** quickly finding and understanding sources on a topic.

> **Research warning:** AI tools sometimes invent facts and even fake citations. Never put a source in your work without opening it and reading the relevant part yourself.

## Writing

### Grammarly
The free version catches spelling, grammar and punctuation mistakes and suggests clearer phrasing, in your browser, in Google Docs and on your phone's keyboard.
**Best for:** polishing essays, emails and applications.

**A note on paraphrasing tools:** apps that "rewrite" text to make it sound different are risky. Using them to disguise someone else's work, or AI-written text, counts as plagiarism at most schools.

## Maths and science

### Wolfram|Alpha
A computational engine that solves equations, plots graphs, converts units and answers science questions with precise results. The free version gives answers; full step-by-step solutions need a paid plan.
**Best for:** checking maths and science work.

**Tip:** for step-by-step help, ask an AI assistant to *explain the method* and then give you a similar problem to solve yourself. See [how to use ChatGPT to study](/guides/how-to-use-chatgpt-to-study/).

## Presentations and design

### Canva
Free design tool for presentations, posters and infographics, with AI features for generating text, images and layouts (the free plan includes limited AI use). Eligible primary and secondary schools can get **Canva for Education** free for teachers and students.
**Best for:** good-looking presentations and projects, fast.

## Coding

### GitHub Student Developer Pack
Free for verified students. It bundles dozens of developer tools and services, including free access to **GitHub Copilot**, an AI coding assistant.
**Best for:** computer science and anyone learning to code.

## Which tools should you pick?

| If you need… | Start with |
|---|---|
| A tutor for any subject | ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude (pick one) |
| To revise from your own slides and notes | NotebookLM |
| To memorise lots of facts | AI-made flashcards in Anki |
| To find sources for an essay | Perplexity, then check every source |
| Cleaner writing | Grammarly |
| To check maths answers | Wolfram\|Alpha |
| A presentation | Canva |
| Help learning to code | GitHub Student Developer Pack |

**Most students need just three:** one AI assistant, NotebookLM, and a flashcard app.

## Use them the right way

- **Check your school's AI policy** and follow it. Rules vary a lot between schools, courses and even assignments.
- **Use AI to learn, not to skip learning.** Ask it to quiz you, explain things and give feedback, not to write your assignments.
- **Protect your privacy.** Don't paste ID numbers, passwords or personal details into AI tools.
- **Check important facts.** AI is often right, but not always.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these tools really free?**
Yes, all have free plans that are enough for most students. Free plans have limits on how much you can use them, and paid upgrades add more. Some companies offer student discounts, which change often, so check their websites.

**Which free AI is best for students?**
There's no single winner. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are all excellent. Try each for a week and keep the one that explains things in a way you like.

**Do I need to pay for AI to get good grades?**
No. How you use AI matters far more than which plan you're on.
