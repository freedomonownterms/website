ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are the three best-known AI assistants, and all three have a free version. They're all genuinely good, and for most everyday tasks any of them will do the job well. But each has its own strengths, and the right choice depends on what you use AI for.

This guide compares them in plain English. We don't list model names or exact message limits: they change every few months, and the companies adjust them often. Instead, we focus on what each one is consistently good at. *Last reviewed: November 2026.*

## The short answer

| If you mostly want to… | Try first |
|---|---|
| Use one all-round assistant for everything | **ChatGPT** |
| Work with Gmail, Google Docs, Drive and Search | **Gemini** |
| Write, edit and work with long documents | **Claude** |
| Use AI inside Windows, Edge and Microsoft 365 | **Microsoft Copilot** |

**Our honest advice:** try two of them for a week each with the tasks you actually do, then keep the one you prefer. It costs nothing, and personal preference matters more than any comparison chart.

## ChatGPT (OpenAI)

**The best-known all-rounder.** ChatGPT has the widest range of features in its free version, including web search, file and image uploads, image generation and voice conversations, with limits on how much you can use each.

**Strong at:**
- General questions, explanations and brainstorming
- A wide mix of tasks in one place, from text and images to voice
- Study help, with a **Study mode** that tutors you with questions instead of answers

**Keep in mind:**
- Heavier features such as image generation and file analysis have tighter free limits
- The free version may show ads in some regions

**Best for:** people who want one app that does a bit of everything.

## Google Gemini

**The best fit if you live in Google's world.** Gemini connects naturally with Google services, and its answers can draw on Google Search.

**Strong at:**
- Tasks connected to Gmail, Docs, Drive, Maps and YouTube
- Up-to-date answers using Google Search
- Research, with a **Deep Research** feature that compiles longer reports
- Learning, with **Guided Learning** for step-by-step help

**Keep in mind:**
- Free access has been adjusted several times, and the most capable models are often reserved for paid plans
- Some features depend on your country, age and account type

**Best for:** Android users and anyone who uses Google apps every day.

## Claude (Anthropic)

**The writer's choice.** Claude is widely praised for natural-sounding writing and for working carefully with long documents.

**Strong at:**
- Writing and editing in a clear, natural voice
- Reading and analysing long PDFs and documents
- Thoughtful feedback on drafts, plans and ideas
- **Projects**, where you can keep documents and instructions together

**Keep in mind:**
- Free usage limits reset every few hours, and heavy users may hit them during busy times
- Fewer extras than ChatGPT, such as image generation

**Best for:** writing, editing, studying long readings and working with documents.

## Microsoft Copilot

Worth mentioning because millions of people already have it. Copilot is free, built into Windows and the Edge browser, and can search the web. If your school or employer uses Microsoft 365, Copilot features may also be available inside Word, Excel, Outlook and PowerPoint, depending on your plan.

**Best for:** Windows users and Microsoft-based workplaces and schools.

## Test them yourself in 10 minutes

The best comparison is your own. Paste these five prompts into two or three assistants and compare the answers:

1. **Explaining:** "Explain how compound interest works using a simple example, then give me one practice question."
2. **Writing:** "Write a friendly 100-word message to my team announcing that our Friday meeting moves to Thursday at 3 pm."
3. **Your real work:** paste a task you actually do, such as an email to answer, a document to summarise or a plan to improve.
4. **Current events:** "What are the most important AI news stories this week? Include sources."
5. **Reasoning:** "I have a 2-hour budget to clean my flat, cook dinner and answer 10 emails. Make me a realistic plan."

Notice which answers you'd actually use with the fewest edits. That's your winner.

## What all of them have in common

- **They can be wrong.** All AI assistants sometimes state incorrect facts with confidence. Check anything important.
- **They're only as good as your prompt.** See our guide to [writing better AI prompts](/guides/how-to-write-better-ai-prompts/).
- **They store your conversations.** Don't paste passwords, ID numbers, bank details or confidential work information, and check each app's privacy settings.
- **Free plans have limits.** Paid plans (typically around $20 a month in the US, with cheaper tiers and regional pricing in some countries) give more usage and the most capable models.

## Should you pay?

For most people, **no, at least not at first.** The free versions handle everyday writing, questions, studying and planning well. Consider paying only if you:

- Regularly hit the free usage limits
- Need the most capable models for complex work, coding or research
- Use AI for work every day

If you do upgrade, pick the one you've already found yourself using most.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI is the most accurate?**
It changes as new models are released, and it depends on the task. All three are strong. For facts that matter, use the one that shows sources and check them yourself.

**Can I use more than one?**
Yes, and many people do: for example, Gemini for quick searches and Claude for writing. They're all free to try.

**Which is best for students?**
All three have learning features. See our guide to the [best free AI tools for students](/guides/best-free-ai-tools-for-students/).

**Which is safest for privacy?**
Each company has different policies and settings. Read the privacy settings in whichever app you use, and turn off chat history or training on your data if you prefer.
