A good resume used to take a weekend. With an AI assistant, you can get a strong first draft in under an hour, and a polished one soon after. The trick is to use AI as a **writing partner that interviews you**, not as a machine that invents a resume for you.

This guide works with any free AI chat tool: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Copilot. Every prompt is ready to copy. Replace the parts in **[brackets]** with your own details.

## Before you start: gather your raw material

AI can only work with what you give it. Spend ten minutes collecting:

- Your **old resume**, if you have one (even an outdated one helps)
- Your **education**: degree, school or college, years, grades if they're good
- **Every job, internship, project, volunteer role or club** you've been part of, even small ones
- **Skills and tools** you can actually use
- **One or two job ads** for the kind of role you want

> **Privacy tip:** remove your phone number, home address and any ID numbers before pasting anything into an AI tool. You can add contact details back at the end.

## Step 1: Let the AI interview you

The biggest mistake people make is typing "write me a resume". You get something generic that could belong to anyone. Instead, ask the AI to ask *you* questions:

> Act as an experienced recruiter. Help me build a one-page resume for a [job title] role. Ask me questions one at a time about my education, projects, internships, skills and achievements. When you have enough, write the resume in clear sections: Summary, Education, Skills, Projects, Experience, Achievements.

Answer honestly and in as much detail as you can. Short answers produce a thin resume; rich answers produce a strong one. If a question doesn't apply to you, say so and move on.

## Step 2: Turn duties into achievements

Most resumes list what someone was *supposed* to do. Recruiters want to know what you actually *achieved*. Paste your rough bullet points and ask:

> Rewrite these resume bullet points so each one starts with a strong action verb and shows a result. Where a number would help, mark the spot with [?] so I can add a real figure. Bullets: [paste your bullets]

Here's the kind of difference this makes:

- **Before:** Responsible for social media posts for the college fest.
- **After:** Planned and posted 40+ Instagram updates for the college fest, growing the page from 800 to 2,300 followers in six weeks.

Notice the **[?]** trick in the prompt. It stops the AI from inventing numbers and reminds you to fill in real ones. If you don't know an exact figure, a fair estimate ("about 30") is fine. A made-up one is not.

## Step 3: Write a summary that sounds like a person

The summary is the two or three lines at the top. It's the first thing a recruiter reads, so it's worth getting right:

> Write 3 different 2–3 line professional summaries for my resume for a [job title] role. My background: [degree, school, year, key skills, best project or job]. Make one formal, one confident and one simple.

Pick the one that sounds most like you, then edit it by hand. Delete clichés like "hard-working team player" and "passionate self-starter". Every applicant says those.

## Step 4: No experience yet? Use what you have

Students and recent graduates often think they have nothing to put on a resume. You almost always have more than you think:

> I'm a student or recent graduate with no work experience. Help me turn my projects, coursework, clubs, volunteering and certifications into strong resume content for a [job title] role. Here's what I've done: [list everything, even small things]

College projects, a family business you helped with, organising an event, tutoring, a YouTube channel, a hackathon: all of these show skills employers want.

## Step 5: Tailor it to each job

One generic resume sent to fifty jobs performs worse than a tailored resume sent to ten. Once you have a strong base resume, adapt it for each application:

> Here is a job description and my resume. List the top 10 skills and keywords the employer wants, show which ones my resume is missing, and rewrite my Summary and Skills sections to match, using only things that are true about me. Job description: [paste] Resume: [paste]

## Step 6: Cut it to one page and proofread

If you have less than ten years of experience, aim for one page. Ask:

> My resume is too long. Cut it to one page for a [job title] role. Tell me what you removed and why. Resume: [paste]

Then run a careful proofread:

> Proofread my resume for grammar, spelling, mixed tenses, inconsistent dates and formatting. List each issue with its fix. Don't rewrite anything else. Resume: [paste]

Asking for a **list of issues** rather than a rewrite keeps you in control and stops the AI from quietly changing things you liked.

## Step 7: Get a brutally honest review

Finally, ask the AI to judge your resume the way a busy recruiter would:

> Act as a busy recruiter who spends 6 seconds on each resume. What do you notice first, what is unclear, and would you shortlist me for [job title]? Be honest.

Fix what it flags, then ask again. Two or three rounds usually gets you to a resume you're proud of.

## Formatting: keep it simple

AI chat tools write text, not designed documents. Paste the final text into a **simple template** in Google Docs or Microsoft Word. For the best results with hiring software:

- Use a **single column**, standard headings (Experience, Education, Skills) and a common font
- Avoid tables, text boxes, icons and photos (unless they're expected in your country)
- Save as **PDF** unless the employer asks for Word

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Never invent experience, skills or numbers.** Interviewers will ask about everything on your resume, and background checks are common. AI should help you present the truth well, not make things up.
2. **Don't send the AI's first draft.** It's a starting point. Your edits are what make it yours.
3. **Watch for repeated words.** AI loves words like "spearheaded", "leveraged" and "dynamic". Vary them, or use plainer language.
4. **Check every fact.** Dates, job titles, company names and grades must be exactly right.
5. **Don't paste private data** such as ID numbers, bank details or passwords into any AI tool.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can employers tell if I used AI to write my resume?**
Sometimes, when the writing is generic and full of buzzwords. A resume built from your real details and edited in your own words reads like any other strong resume. See our guide on [whether it's OK to use AI for job applications](/guides/is-it-ok-to-use-ai-for-job-applications/).

**Is ChatGPT free for this?**
Yes. The free versions of ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot are all good enough to write and improve a resume.

**Which AI is best for resumes?**
Any of the major assistants will do the job well. What matters far more is the quality of the information and instructions you give it.
