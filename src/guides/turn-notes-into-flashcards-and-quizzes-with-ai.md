Two of the most effective ways to study are **testing yourself** and **reviewing material at spaced intervals**. Flashcards do both. The problem has always been the time it takes to make them: writing a hundred cards by hand can take longer than learning the material.

AI fixes that. In a few minutes, it can turn your notes, slides or a textbook chapter into flashcards and practice quizzes, ready to use or import into an app.

All prompts work with ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot. Replace the parts in **[brackets]** with your own details.

## Method 1: Flashcards from your notes (any AI chat)

Paste your notes, or upload a PDF or photo if your AI tool supports it, and use:

> Create 20 flashcards from the notes below. Each card should test one fact or idea. Put the question on the front and a short answer (under 25 words) on the back. Cover the most important concepts, definitions and formulas, and avoid trivial details. Format them as a two-column table: Front | Back. Notes: [paste]

**Why the rules matter:**
- **One idea per card.** Cards that test several things at once are hard to review and easy to half-know.
- **Short answers.** If the answer is a paragraph, split the card in two.
- **"Avoid trivial details."** Otherwise you get cards about page numbers and minor examples.

## Method 2: Fill-in-the-blank cards

For definitions, laws and key sentences, fill-in-the-blank cards (sometimes called **cloze** cards) work brilliantly:

> Turn these key sentences into fill-in-the-blank flashcards. Replace the single most important word or phrase in each sentence with "_____" and put the missing word on the back. Notes: [paste]

**Example:**
- **Front:** Photosynthesis converts light energy into _____ energy stored in glucose.
- **Back:** chemical

## Method 3: Practice quizzes

Flashcards test recall. Quizzes test whether you can **apply** what you know:

> Create a 10-question practice quiz on [topic] based on the notes below. Mix multiple-choice, true/false and short-answer questions, from easy to hard. Don't show the answers until I've answered all 10. Then mark my answers and explain each mistake. Notes: [paste]

Answer honestly, without peeking. Then ask for a second quiz focused only on what you got wrong:

> Make 5 new questions on the topics I got wrong.

## Method 4: Use NotebookLM for your own sources

**NotebookLM** is a free Google tool that answers using **only the sources you upload**, such as PDFs, slides, Google Docs, websites or YouTube videos. That makes it a strong choice for studying, because it stays close to your course material and shows which source each answer comes from.

1. Go to notebooklm.google.com and sign in with a Google account.
2. Create a notebook and upload your lecture slides, notes or readings.
3. In the **Studio** panel, choose **Flashcards** or **Quiz** to generate them from your sources.

It can also produce study guides and audio overviews, a podcast-style discussion of your material that's handy for revising on the go. The free plan has daily usage limits, and features change, so check what's currently available.

## Import your cards into a flashcard app

A flashcard app shows each card **just before you're likely to forget it**. This is called spaced repetition, and it's what makes flashcards so powerful over weeks of study.

**Anki** (free on Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and the web; the iPhone app is paid):

> Convert these flashcards into a plain text list with one card per line, the front and back separated by a tab character, with no header row.

Save the result as a `.txt` file, then in Anki choose **File → Import**, select the file, and confirm that the fields are separated by tabs.

**Quizlet** (free plan available):

Create a new set, choose **Import**, and paste the same tab-separated list. Quizlet will split each line into a term and a definition.

## Always check the cards

AI sometimes gets facts wrong, especially numbers, dates, names and formulas. Before you start reviewing:

- **Skim every card** against your notes or textbook.
- **Delete** any card you don't understand or don't need.
- **Rewrite** cards in your own words where it helps. Editing them is itself a form of studying.

## A simple weekly routine

| When | What |
|---|---|
| After each lecture or chapter | Generate 15–20 cards, check them, import them |
| Every day | Review due cards in your app (10–20 minutes) |
| End of each week | Take an AI practice quiz on the week's topics |
| Before the exam | Quiz yourself only on topics you keep getting wrong |

## Frequently asked questions

**How many flashcards should I make?**
Fewer, better cards beat hundreds of weak ones. Start with 15–20 per lecture or chapter, covering only what you'd expect in an exam.

**Is it better to make flashcards by hand?**
Writing cards yourself helps you learn, but it's slow. A good middle ground is to let AI draft them, then edit and rewrite them yourself.

**Can AI make flashcards from a photo of my notes?**
Many AI apps can read photos and handwriting. Results depend on how clear the writing is, so check the cards carefully.
