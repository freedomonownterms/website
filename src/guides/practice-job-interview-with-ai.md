Most people prepare for interviews by reading lists of common questions. That helps, but it's not the same as **answering out loud, under a little pressure, and getting honest feedback**. That's what makes people better at interviews, and until recently it needed a friend, a mentor or a paid coach.

Now any free AI assistant can play the interviewer. It can ask realistic questions, wait for your answer, score it, and show you how to improve, as many times as you like and at any hour.

Every prompt below works with ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot. Replace the parts in **[brackets]** with your own details.

## Step 1: Give the AI the job description

Interviewers ask questions based on the job. So should your AI. Start by pasting the job ad:

> List the 20 questions I'm most likely to be asked in an interview for [job title] at [company], based on this job description. Group them into HR, technical and role-specific questions. Job description: [paste]

Read through the list. Mark the questions that make you nervous. Those are the ones to practise first.

## Step 2: Run a full mock interview

This is the core of the method. Paste this prompt, then answer each question as if you were in the room:

> Act as an interviewer for a [job title] role at a [type of company]. Ask me one question at a time, mixing HR, technical and situational questions. After each answer, rate it out of 10 and tell me how to improve it. Stop after 10 questions and give overall feedback.

A few tips to make it realistic:

- **Answer before you think too long.** Real interviews don't give you five minutes per question.
- **Don't edit your typed answer.** Write it the way you'd say it, mistakes included.
- **Ask for a tougher interviewer** once you're comfortable: add "Be strict and ask follow-up questions when my answers are vague."

## Step 3: Practise by speaking, not just typing

Interviews are spoken, so practise speaking. Most major AI apps on phones now have a **voice mode**, where you talk and the AI talks back. Use the same mock interview prompt in voice mode and you'll get much closer to the real experience.

If voice mode isn't available to you, read your typed answers out loud before sending them, or record yourself on your phone and listen back. You'll quickly notice filler words, rambling and answers that are too short.

## Step 4: Nail "Tell me about yourself"

It's the most common opening question, and the one people prepare least. Build a strong answer with:

> Write a 60–90 second answer to "Tell me about yourself" for a [job title] interview, using a present–past–future structure. My background: [paste]. Then give me a shorter 30-second version.

**Present–past–future** means: what you do now, what you've done that's relevant, and why this job is the right next step. Practise it out loud until it feels natural, not memorised.

## Step 5: Prepare your stories with STAR

Behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time when…") are where many candidates struggle. The fix is to prepare four or five true stories in advance using the **STAR** structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

> Turn this experience into a STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) answer that takes under 2 minutes to say, for questions about [teamwork / leadership / pressure / conflict]: [describe what happened]

One good story can often answer several questions. A story about rescuing a late group project could cover teamwork, pressure, problem-solving *and* leadership.

## Step 6: Get ready for technical questions

For technical or skills-based roles, ask the AI to quiz you:

> Quiz me on [topic, e.g. SQL joins / Excel / Python basics / accounting]. Ask one question at a time, from easy to hard. If I get one wrong, explain the right answer simply before moving on.

If you're likely to be asked about a project on your resume, prepare for that too:

> I'll be asked about my project: [describe it]. Ask me the 10 toughest questions an interviewer could ask about it, one at a time, and help me improve each of my answers.

## Step 7: Prepare questions to ask them

At the end of almost every interview you'll hear "Do you have any questions for us?" Saying "no" is a missed opportunity.

> Give me 10 smart questions to ask at the end of a [job title] interview at a [startup / large company / government] organisation, and explain what each one helps me learn.

Pick two or three that genuinely interest you.

## Step 8: Improve your spoken English (optional)

If you're interviewing in English as a second language, AI makes an excellent, patient practice partner:

> Help me practise speaking for interviews. Ask me a common interview question. I'll type my answer the way I'd say it. Then correct my grammar, suggest more natural phrasing, and show me a polished version. Repeat with a new question.

## A simple one-week practice plan

| Day | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generate likely questions from the job description | 20 min |
| 2 | Write and practise "Tell me about yourself" | 30 min |
| 3 | Build 4–5 STAR stories | 45 min |
| 4 | Full mock interview, typed | 30 min |
| 5 | Technical quiz or project questions | 30 min |
| 6 | Full mock interview in voice mode, strict interviewer | 30 min |
| 7 | Review feedback, prepare your questions, rest | 20 min |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Memorising AI-written answers word for word.** You'll sound robotic, and you'll freeze if the question is phrased differently. Learn the key points, not the script.
2. **Only practising easy questions.** Ask the AI to be tough. It's better to struggle at home than in the room.
3. **Trusting every technical answer.** AI can be confidently wrong. If an explanation doesn't match your notes or textbooks, check a reliable source.
4. **Exaggerating in your stories.** Interviewers ask follow-up questions. Keep every story true.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is AI interview practice really useful?**
Yes, for the same reason any practice is: you get comfortable saying your answers out loud and you get feedback on them. It's not a perfect substitute for a human mock interview, but it's free, available anytime and endlessly patient.

**Can AI help with group discussions?**
Yes. Ask it for the main points for and against your topic, a few strong opening lines, and tips for contributing without dominating. The full prompt is in our free kit.

**What should I do after the interview?**
Send a short thank-you email within a day. AI can draft it in seconds; just make sure you mention something specific from your conversation.
