Everyone's first resume starts the same way: an empty "Experience" section and the feeling that you have nothing to write. The good news is that recruiters hiring for entry-level roles don't expect a job history. They're looking for **evidence that you can learn, take initiative and get things done**, and you almost certainly have that already.

An AI assistant is very good at one thing here: spotting the skills hidden in what you've done and putting them into clear resume language. This guide shows you how, step by step, with free prompts you can fill in and copy.

## What counts as experience when you've never had a job

More than you think. All of these belong on a first resume:

- **College or school projects**, especially group projects and anything with a result.
- **Clubs, societies, events and sports**, particularly if you organised or led something.
- **Volunteering**, tutoring and community work.
- **Certificates and online courses**, free ones included.
- **Freelance or family-business help**, from social media posts to accounts.
- **Personal projects**: a blog, a small app, a design portfolio, a YouTube channel.

If you're not sure what you've done, start with the free [Find hidden achievements](/prompts/resumes/find-hidden-achievements/) prompt. The AI asks you questions one at a time until you remember things you'd forgotten.

## Step 1: List everything, even small things

Before you open an AI tool, write a rough list. Don't polish it. For each item, note **what you did** and, if you can, **a number**: how many people, how many weeks, how much growth.

For example: "Ran Instagram for our college festival, grew it from 300 to 2,100 followers. Group project: market research on 12 local cafés. Google Digital Marketing certificate. Volunteer English tutor every Saturday."

Numbers are what make a beginner's resume stand out, so dig for them.

## Step 2: Let AI turn your list into resume content

Open the free [No experience yet](/prompts/resumes/no-experience-yet/) prompt. Type the job title and your list into the boxes, then copy the finished prompt or open it straight in ChatGPT or Claude.

We tested this exact prompt in a video. ChatGPT produced a full resume in seconds: a summary, education, projects written as marketing experience, volunteering and a skills section. It also **kept the real numbers** we gave it, which is exactly what you want.

## Step 3: Delete anything that isn't true

This is the step most people skip, and it matters most. In our test, the AI also **added things we never told it**: a list of coursework, and vague claims like "contributed to identifying market trends".

AI fills gaps with plausible-sounding text. On a resume, that's a problem, because an interviewer can ask about any line. Go through the draft and:

- **Delete** anything you didn't do.
- **Replace** vague claims with what actually happened.
- **Fill in** every placeholder, like [Your name], [College] and [Year].

## Step 4: Make your strongest project shine

Pick your best project and give it three sharp bullet points. The free [Showcase a project](/prompts/resumes/showcase-a-project/) prompt turns a description into bullets that show the problem, what you built, the tools you used and the result.

If your bullets still sound flat, run them through [Upgrade weak bullet points](/prompts/resumes/upgrade-weak-bullet-points/). It starts each one with a strong action verb and marks where a number would help.

## Step 5: Write a summary and organise your skills

A two- or three-line summary at the top tells a recruiter who you are in seconds. Use [Write a sharp summary](/prompts/resumes/write-a-sharp-summary/) to get three versions, then pick the one that sounds most like you.

Then use [Organise your skills](/prompts/resumes/organise-your-skills/) to group your skills into clear categories and drop anything weak or irrelevant.

## Step 6: Test it like a recruiter would

Before you send it anywhere:

1. Run [The 6-second recruiter test](/prompts/resumes/the-6-second-recruiter-test/). The AI tells you what a busy recruiter notices first and what's unclear.
2. Use [Proofread like an editor](/prompts/resumes/proofread-like-an-editor/) to catch spelling, tense and date mistakes.
3. If it runs over a page, [Cut it to one page](/prompts/resumes/cut-it-to-one-page/).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sending the AI draft as it is.** Always edit it, and keep only what's true.
- **Hiding your best work at the bottom.** For beginners, projects often deserve to come before education.
- **Listing duties instead of results.** "Managed Instagram" is weaker than "Grew Instagram from 300 to 2,100 followers".
- **One resume for every job.** Adjust it for each role. Our guide to [tailoring your resume to a job description](/guides/tailor-resume-to-job-description-with-ai/) shows how.
- **Pasting private details into AI tools.** Leave out your phone number, address and ID numbers, and add them yourself at the end.

## The bottom line

No work experience doesn't mean an empty resume. Write down everything you've done, let AI turn it into clear resume language, then **check every line yourself**. You'll end up with a resume that's honest, specific and ready to send.
