Sending application after application and hearing nothing back is one of the most discouraging parts of a job search. The silence usually doesn't mean you're not good enough. Much more often, something in **how** you apply is stopping your application before a person reads it properly.

Here are seven common reasons, how to spot each one, and a free AI prompt that helps you fix it.

## First, find out which problem you have

Start with the free [Why am I not getting calls?](/prompts/job-search/why-am-i-not-getting-calls/) prompt. Describe the roles you're applying for, how you apply and how many applications you've sent. The AI lists the most likely reasons and what to change first. Then use the fixes below.

## 1. Your resume doesn't match the job

The most common problem. If your resume doesn't use the skills and words in the job description, both screening software and recruiters may pass over it.

**Fix:** adjust your resume for each role. Paste the job description and your resume into the [Tailor your resume to a job](/prompts/resumes/tailor-your-resume-to-a-job/) prompt. It shows which keywords you're missing and rewrites your summary and skills to match, using only what's true about you. Our guide to [tailoring a resume with AI](/guides/tailor-resume-to-job-description-with-ai/) walks you through it.

## 2. Screening software can't read it

Many employers use an applicant tracking system (ATS) to sort applications. Fancy layouts with tables, columns, icons or text in headers can confuse it.

**Fix:** use a simple one-column layout with standard headings like "Experience" and "Education". The [Pass the ATS screen](/prompts/resumes/pass-the-ats-screen/) prompt checks your resume for formatting problems and missing keywords.

## 3. The first few seconds don't impress

Recruiters often skim a resume very quickly before deciding whether to read on. If your strongest point is buried, they may never see it.

**Fix:** put your best result near the top, in your summary or first bullet. Run [The 6-second recruiter test](/prompts/resumes/the-6-second-recruiter-test/) to see what a busy recruiter notices first.

## 4. Your bullet points list duties, not results

"Responsible for social media" says what your job was, not what you achieved.

**Fix:** rewrite each bullet as action plus result, with a number where you can: "Grew Instagram from 300 to 2,100 followers in four months." The [Upgrade weak bullet points](/prompts/resumes/upgrade-weak-bullet-points/) prompt does this and marks where you should add a real figure.

## 5. You're applying to the wrong roles

Applying for jobs that need much more experience, or for titles that don't fit your skills, leads to silence.

**Fix:** before applying, use [Decode a job description](/prompts/job-search/decode-a-job-description/) to see what the employer really needs and whether you're a match. Then try [Discover roles that fit you](/prompts/job-search/discover-roles-that-fit-you/) to find job titles you may not have considered.

## 6. You only apply through job boards

Popular job-board listings can get a very large number of applicants. If that's your only channel, you're competing with everyone else.

**Fix:** add more personal routes:

- Ask people you know for referrals. The [Ask for a referral](/prompts/applications/ask-for-a-referral/) prompt writes a polite, short message.
- Contact hiring managers directly with a [Cold email to a hiring manager](/prompts/applications/cold-email-to-a-hiring-manager/).
- Follow up a week or so after applying with [Follow up on an application](/prompts/applications/follow-up-on-an-application/).

## 7. Some of the jobs aren't real

Fake listings and scams target job seekers, especially for remote work. Applying to them wastes time and can put your personal data at risk.

**Fix:** be wary of jobs that ask for payment, offer very high pay for little work, or rush you to share ID or bank details. Paste a suspicious ad into the [Spot a fake job](/prompts/job-search/spot-a-fake-job/) prompt for a second opinion. Our guide to [spotting AI scams and deepfakes](/guides/how-to-spot-ai-scams-and-deepfakes/) covers more warning signs.

## Keep track, and keep going

Note where you applied, when, and what happened, so you can spot what's working. The [Track every application](/prompts/applications/track-every-application/) prompt sets up a simple tracker for you. And if you'd like a structure for the weeks ahead, the [4-week job search plan](/prompts/job-search/4-week-job-search-plan/) prompt builds one around your goals.

## The bottom line

Silence is usually a fixable problem, not a verdict on you. Find out which of the seven reasons applies, fix that first, and track the results. Every prompt here is free in the [AI Prompt Library](/prompts/), and each one remembers your details for next time.
