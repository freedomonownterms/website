You no longer need design skills, or money, to create images with AI. Several excellent tools let you generate pictures, illustrations, social media graphics and more for free. The catch: every free plan has limits, and each tool is good at different things.

This guide compares the most popular free options by what they're best at. We don't list exact daily limits. They change often, and published numbers frequently disagree, so check each tool's own pricing page for the current allowance. *Last reviewed: November 2026.*

## The tools in more detail

### ChatGPT
ChatGPT's image generation understands long, detailed instructions well, and you can **refine images through conversation**: "make the sky darker", "add a dog on the left", "now make it look like a watercolour". The free plan only allows a small number of images, so it's best for occasional use.

### Google Gemini
Gemini creates and edits images directly in the chat. It's particularly handy for **editing your own photos with simple instructions**, such as changing a background or style. It's a natural choice if you already use Google apps.

### Microsoft Copilot and Designer
Free with a Microsoft account, built into Copilot and the Microsoft Designer app. A simple, no-fuss option for quick images.

### Adobe Firefly
Adobe says Firefly is trained on licensed content such as Adobe Stock and public-domain material, which makes it a popular choice for **businesses worried about copyright**. It also connects with Photoshop and Adobe Express. Free users get a limited number of monthly credits.

### Canva
Canva's strength isn't just generating images. It's **turning them into finished designs** using its huge library of templates for social posts, posters, presentations and more. Its AI tools are limited on the free plan.

### Ideogram
Most AI image tools struggle to spell words correctly inside images. Ideogram is known for being much better at it, which makes it useful for **posters, logos, quotes and signs**.

### Leonardo
Popular with creators and game designers for its **range of artistic styles** and controls. Its free daily credits are enough to experiment.

## Which should you use?

- **Just want a quick image?** Use the AI assistant you already have: ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot.
- **Need text in the image?** Try Ideogram.
- **Making a social post, poster or slide?** Generate the image, then finish it in Canva.
- **Using images for a business?** Consider Adobe Firefly, and read the terms of any tool you use.
- **Want artistic control?** Try Leonardo.

## How to write image prompts that work

Vague prompts produce generic images. Describe the picture like you're briefing a photographer or illustrator:

1. **Subject:** what's in the image
2. **Setting:** where it is and what's around it
3. **Style:** photo, watercolour, 3D render, flat illustration, pencil sketch
4. **Lighting and mood:** golden hour, soft studio light, dramatic, cosy
5. **Composition:** close-up, wide shot, from above, centred, space for text
6. **Format:** square for Instagram, wide for a banner or slide, tall for a phone

**Example:**
> A cosy independent bookshop on a rainy evening, warm yellow light glowing from the window, a cat sleeping on a stack of books by the door, watercolour illustration style, soft muted colours, wide format with empty space at the top for a title.

If the result isn't right, change **one thing at a time** and try again.

## Use AI images responsibly

**Check the usage rights.** Each tool's terms say how you can use the images, especially commercially. Read them before using an image for a business, product or advertisement.

**Copyright is still evolving.** In many countries, purely AI-generated images may not be protected by copyright in the same way as human-created work, so others may be able to reuse them. If you need exclusive rights, such as for a logo, use AI for ideas and have a designer create the final version.

**Avoid real people and brands.** Don't create images of real people without their permission, and don't use other companies' logos or characters.

**Never present AI images as real.** Using AI images to mislead, such as fake news photos, fake product results or fake evidence, can be harmful and in some places illegal. Learn how to [spot AI fakes and scams](/guides/how-to-spot-ai-scams-and-deepfakes/).

**Label AI images when it matters,** for example in news, education or anywhere people might assume an image is a real photo.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free AI image generator is best?**
There's no single best. For most people, the image feature in the AI assistant they already use is enough. For text in images, Ideogram stands out; for business use, Adobe Firefly's licensing approach is reassuring.

**Can I use free AI images commercially?**
Often yes, but it depends on each tool's terms, which can differ between free and paid plans. Always check before using images in products or ads.

**Why do AI images get hands and text wrong?**
These are hard things for image models to get right. Tools are improving quickly, but always check details before publishing.
